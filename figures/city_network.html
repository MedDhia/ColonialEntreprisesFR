Where the empire's boards met

Every other figure in this repository places a firm by whom it is connected to. This one places it by where it was — city coordinates, plate carrée — and draws an edge between two cities when a director sat on a board in each. The unit is the city rather than the colony, because filing a firm under Indochine hides that Saigon and Hanoi were largely separate business worlds, and filing it under Maroc hides that its board met in Paris.

Paris is the finding, not an artefact. 41% of the placed firms in the interlock graph were run from Paris — more than the next 21 cities together — and the densest lines on the map run from Paris outward rather than between colonies. Colonial business was administered from the metropole.

Read with three limits in mind. Coverage is partial: 1,393 of the 3,085 firms in the interlock graph have a recoverable address (45%), and the rest are absent from this figure entirely. A head office is not an operation: a plantation in Cochinchina run from a Paris office appears at Paris, which is a real fact about control but not about where the work happened. Ties within one city cannot be drawn — an edge from Paris to Paris is a dot — so the 3,275 within-city interlocks are in the table's last column, not on the map, against 7,808 drawn between cities. There is no coastline because no basemap ships with this repository; the graticule and the labels carry the geography.
